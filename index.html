Section 0 — Existing Work (Starting Point)

0A — PTC Lettermark (Ink + Clay) Serif + Sans
Instrument Serif + Inter · Clay #C26244 · Hairline rule separator
Elegant serif has warmth and editorial personality. The hairline rule is a nice detail — refined without being precious. Clay is distinctive and correct for the brand.
Open question: Does Instrument Serif read "experienced consulting" or "boutique artisan"? Test at small sizes (business card, email signature).

The period is a deliberate design element — it says finality, clarity, no ambiguity. Conceptually perfect for a firm named "Plain Terms." The name literally ends with a full stop. This is the Nike play: says what it is, stops there. No decoration needed.
Strengths: Most conceptually tight. Distinctive at a glance. Scales to all sizes well.
Watch: Some clients may not read the period as intentional — it needs consistent application to land.

Bold stacked type with tight negative tracking. The compressed all-caps feels decisive and structured — PwC's credibility, Altra's no-nonsense quality. Left-aligned is more editorial and interesting than centered.
Strengths: Very strong on presentations, decks, letterhead. Confident.
Watch: All-caps can feel cold. Test whether it still reads "warm" with the clay accent doing enough work.

A clean horizontal lockup with a vertical pipe separator. Calm, professional, approachable — closest to Altra's register. Sentence case feels human vs. all-caps authority.
Strengths: Most approachable. Scales cleanly to email signature, footer, header.
Watch: Least distinctive of the three — could look like many other horizontal lockups.

Bold geometric sans, tightly kerned — "PT" reads as a single unit. Works as a social avatar, app icon, email signature mark, or embossed element on materials.
Strengths: Extremely scalable. Dark background version is very strong. Pairs well with any wordmark above.
